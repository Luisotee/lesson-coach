# Solving linear equations

## The idea

An equation says that two expressions have the same value. `2*x + 6 = 10` says that two lots of a number, plus six, make ten. Solving it means finding the number that makes the statement true.

Think of a balance scale. The two sides weigh the same. Whatever you do to one side, you must do to the other, and the scale stays level. We write the operation in the margin, for example "| -6", so it is clear that both sides change.

## Why it works

To get `x` alone, undo what was done to it, in reverse order. In `2*x + 6 = 10`, the number was first doubled and then six was added. So we first subtract six from both sides, `2*x = 4`, and then divide both sides by two, `x = 2`.

Each step makes an equation with the same solution, because doing the same thing to equal amounts keeps them equal. Check the answer by putting it back in: `2*2 + 6` is `10`, so `x = 2` is right.

Brackets come first. In `5*(x + 3) = 35`, the five multiplies everything in the bracket. Either divide both sides by five, `x + 3 = 7`, or multiply out, `5*x + 15 = 35`. Both lead to `x = 4`.

## Common misconceptions

Some students move a number across the equals sign without changing its sign. They turn `2*x + 6 = 10` into `2*x = 16`. Subtracting six from both sides gives `2*x = 4`; nothing was added.

Some students divide only one term by the number in front of `x`. From `2*x + 6 = 10` they write `x + 6 = 5`, dividing the `2*x` and the `10` but not the `6`. Dividing a side means dividing every term on it.

Some students subtract the number in front of `x` instead of dividing by it. They turn `3*x = 12` into `x = 9`. But `3*x` means three times `x`, so we undo it by dividing by three.

Some students undo the steps in the wrong order. They divide first and subtract later, writing `x = 10/2 - 6`, which is `-1`. Undoing in reverse order means the last thing done to `x` is undone first.

Some students multiply the number outside a bracket by the first term only. They write `5*(x + 3) = 35` as `5*x + 3 = 35`. The five multiplies everything inside the bracket.

## What to ask

Ask students to write the margin operation on every line and to check their answer by substituting it. A check that fails tells them a step went wrong, without anyone telling them they were wrong.
