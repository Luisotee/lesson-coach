# Negative numbers

## The idea

A negative number is a number below zero. On a number line, `-3` sits three steps to the left of zero, just as `3` sits three steps to the right. Every number has an opposite at the same distance from zero on the other side.

Adding a positive number moves right on the number line. Adding a negative number moves left. Subtracting a number moves the opposite way to adding it, so subtracting a negative number moves right.

## Why it works

Subtraction asks "what is the difference?" The difference between `5` and `-3` is the whole distance from `-3` up to `5`, which is eight steps. So `5 - (-3)` is `8`. Subtracting a negative is the same as adding its opposite.

For multiplication, look at a pattern. `3*(-2)` is `-6`, `2*(-2)` is `-4`, `1*(-2)` is `-2` and `0*(-2)` is `0`. Each time the first number drops by one, the answer goes up by two. Carry on: `-1*(-2)` is `2` and `-2*(-2)` is `4`. A negative times a negative is positive because the pattern must continue.

When two numbers have different signs, adding them is a tug of war. The answer takes the sign of the number further from zero, and its size is the difference of the two sizes. So `-5 + 3` is `-2`.

## Common misconceptions

Some students believe that subtracting a negative number makes the answer smaller. They see two minus signs and keep the minus, so `5 - (-3)` becomes `2`. Taking away a debt leaves you richer, not poorer.

Some students believe that a negative times a negative is negative. They copy the sign they see, so `(-2)*(-3)` becomes `-6`. The multiplication pattern shows the answer has to be positive.

Some students add the sizes and keep the sign of the first number. They treat `-5 + 3` as `-8`, as if both numbers pulled the same way. The positive three pulls back towards zero.

Some students always subtract the smaller number from the larger one. They write `3 - 7` as `4` because "you can't take seven from three". On the number line, seven steps left from three lands on `-4`.

Some students apply "two negatives make a positive" to addition. They write `-4 + (-3)` as `7`. The rule is about multiplying and dividing; adding two negatives goes further below zero.

## What to ask

Ask students to show each calculation as a move on a number line. Ask them to say "subtract" and "negative" as separate words, so the operation and the sign of the number stay apart.
